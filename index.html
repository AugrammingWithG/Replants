Saved from the bulldozer, one tree at a time

Bruce Abbott started Replants in 2000 because whole blocks of Perth bush were being scraped bare for new suburbs. We work with developers and landowners to get the grass trees, zamias and understorey out before the machines go in, and we’ve moved thousands of grass trees into gardens and landscape projects since.

How we clear a site

  1. Keep as much natural vegetation standing as the plan allows.
  2. Transplant the valuable plants: grass trees, zamias, understorey.
  3. Mill jarrah, marri, tuart, banksia and sheoak for timber and firewood.
  4. Mulch green waste into mulch and compost.
  5. Relocate the native animals living there.

Clearing your own block for a house or shed? We salvage from small lots in the Perth hills and the South West, and it can cut your clearing bill. Call Bruce on 0408 885 435 or email bruce@replants.com.

Plant it right, once

Grass trees grow about 12 mm of trunk a year, so choose one that suits the space now. Give it sun, free-draining sand, and water at the roots, never on the leaves.

  • Sun. Open, sunny spots are best; part shade is fine.
  • Sand, not clay. On heavy soil, add gypsum to the bottom of the hole and mound coarse sand so there’s at least 500 mm under and around the roots.
  • Room to spread. A single head fills 1–1.5 m across; keep trunks at least 1.5 m apart.
  • Mulch, with a gap. Keep it 150 mm clear of the trunk. Skip pine bark.
  • Water the roots. Drippers or bubblers into the moat. Don’t let sprinklers wet the leaves.
  • Clean the hole. Replacing an old tree or palm? Dig out every bit of the old roots first.

What the first two years look like

Brown tips in the first months are normal. Here’s what to expect, so you know when to relax and when to call.

  1. 0–6 months

    Settling in

    Roots grow fast. Leaf tips, and sometimes the outer layer of foliage, go brown. Don’t worry.

  2. 6–12 months

    Establishing

    New roots chase deep water down. Yellow shoots appear in the centre and turn green in the sun.

  3. 12–24 months

    Established

    New foliage takes over and old leaves form a dry skirt. Keep deep-watering through hot spells.

  4. 24 months on

    Self-reliant

    It finds its own water. Trim the skirt if you like, to show the black trunk again.

Looking stressed? Four checks

Is the new growth in the centre healthy?

If it is, brown outer leaves are likely just the heat. If not, the tree is stressed: apply Seasol or Ecovital over the foliage and around the base once a month. Healthy trees benefit too.

Is it getting enough water?

In hot months, water 2–3 times a week with about 50 L each time. In cooler months, 1–2 times a week. Skip it when it’s raining a lot.

Is it getting too much?

Check mulch isn’t piled against the trunk, and that the black trunk at ground level is dry and firm, not soft. If it’s soft, clear the mulch and sand away from it.

Are there bugs or powder on the leaves?

Look low in the foliage. White powder is mildew; small grey-white bugs are mealybugs. Spray with Mancozeb or white oil, but not in hot weather.

12-month warranty

When you plant with Replants Transplant Formula and follow our care guide, we’ll replace a tree that doesn’t make it with a like tree for 25% of the original price, plus delivery and installation if needed.

The conditions
  • Planted using the Replants transplanting method.
  • Watered as we advise, by dripper, bubbler or flooder at the base.
  • Foliage kept dry: no regular sprinkler spray.
  • The moat around the base kept in shape.
  • Formula reapplied 6 months after planting.
  • Flower spikes removed at the first flowering.
